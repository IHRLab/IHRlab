Proto-OKN Theme 1: Dream-KG

Develop Dynamic, REsponsive, Adaptive, and Multifaceted Knowledge Graphs to address homelessness with Explainable AI

Dream-KG is building a knowledge graph to support homelessness triage, service coordination, and data integration across sectors — co-designed with a Community Advisory Board.

Principal Investigator: Huanmei Wu, PhD

Co-Investigators: Chiu Tan, PhD; Yuzhou Chen, PhD; Ying Ding, PhD; Philip McCallion, PhD; Omar Martinez, JD, MPH, MS

Sponsor: National Science Foundation

Timeline: 10/01/2023 – 09/30/2026

Project focus

This project is developing a knowledge graph that provides a comprehensive understanding of the social, economic, and political factors that contribute to homelessness while triaging existing resources. It is developed in partnership with a Community Advisory Board of social-service providers, clinicians, nonprofit leaders, policy advocates, and individuals with lived experience — spanning structural factors, evidence-based interventions, community resources, cultural nuance, and policy from local to federal levels.

Never Again: Advancing the Prevention of Recurrent Child Sexual Abuse

Identifying drivers of recurrent childhood sexual abuse to inform innovative, targeted, systems-level prevention strategies.

Principal Investigator: Julia Kobulsky, PhD

Co-Investigators: Chang Su, PhD; Huanmei Wu, PhD; Krista Schroeder, PhD; Kevin Henry, PhD; Jingwei Wu, PhD

Sponsor / Award: NIH / NICHD (Eunice Kennedy Shriver National Institute of Child Health and Human Development) · Weill Cornell Medical College

Timeline: 09/20/2024 – 08/19/2028

Introduction

While much research focuses on identifying abuse and treatment after harm occurs, far less is known about why abuse recurs and how systems can intervene earlier. Using a unique, linked longitudinal dataset of child-welfare records and law-enforcement investigations in Philadelphia, the team applies natural language processing to case narratives and geospatial analyses of neighborhood context, interpreted through qualitative interviews with practitioners, caregivers, and survivors — translating insights into practical, trauma-informed, system-ready prevention approaches.

Co-Investigators: Ben Gerber, MD, MPH; Huanmei Wu, PhD; Omar Martinez, JD, MPH, MS

Sponsor: NIH / NHLBI

Timeline: 04/20/2024 – 03/31/2028

Introduction

Cardiovascular-disease risk is shaped not only by clinical factors but by social and structural conditions that influence access to care and prevention — factors existing risk models largely ignore. AI2Equity develops an AI-enhanced, equitable CVD risk model that integrates clinical data with multi-level social drivers of health, using advanced machine-learning methods and diverse datasets to improve prediction and inform interventions that reduce disparities in outcomes.

Enhancing Trial Capacity at Temple University ALS Center of Hope

Expanding clinical-trial capacity while reducing participation burden and advancing equitable enrollment of individuals with ALS from racial and ethnic minority populations.

Principal Investigator: Terry Heiman-Patterson, MD

Co-Investigators: Huanmei Wu, PhD

Sponsor: The ALS Association

Timeline: 01/01/2023 – 12/31/2026

Introduction

Temple's ALS Center of Hope runs clinical trials but has found racial and ethnic minority populations underrepresented. This project addresses three key barriers — the high opportunity costs of participation, delayed referrals, and mistrust of research — by integrating trial visits with clinic visits; providing support for parking, transportation, and meals; educating providers on bias, early diagnosis, and timely referral; and educating community members and people with ALS about clinical trials and participant protections.

Improving Preventive Care Through Data-Driven Insights for Chronic Disease Management

Ensuring that patients with chronic conditions like hypertension and type-2 diabetes receive consistent preventive care — using AI and health-information-exchange data to identify who is at risk of falling out of care.

Funder: Robert Wood Johnson Foundation (RWJF) Health Data for Action Initiative

Grant Number: 78961

Project Overview

This project addresses a critical challenge in U.S. healthcare: ensuring that patients with chronic conditions like hypertension and type-2 diabetes receive consistent preventive care. Despite the availability of effective treatments, many patients struggle to maintain regular healthcare visits, leading to increased emergency-department use, complications, and healthcare costs.

Research Challenge

Understanding why some patients adhere to recommended care visits while others do not — and why some resort to emergency departments for conditions manageable through routine care — requires analyzing complex interactions between patient characteristics, social determinants of health, healthcare-access factors, and provider-level influences. Traditional approaches often examine these factors in isolation, missing patterns that emerge from comprehensive data integration.

Our Approach

Leveraging data from the HealthShare Exchange, a regional health-information exchange serving multiple healthcare systems, this research examines patient- and provider-level factors associated with preventive-care visit adherence and emergency-department utilization among adults with hypertension, type-2 diabetes, or both. The project employs advanced machine-learning and AI methods to develop predictive models that identify patients at risk for poor care adherence or inappropriate ED use.

Key Research Questions

  • What patient-level factors (demographics, comorbidities, social determinants) influence preventive-care visit adherence?
  • How do provider-level and healthcare-system factors affect care patterns?
  • Can machine-learning models accurately predict which patients are at highest risk for care non-adherence or ED overutilization?
  • What actionable insights can inform targeted interventions by health systems and insurers?

Impact & Significance

This work aims to give health systems and insurers evidence-based strategies for improving maintenance care for chronic diseases. By identifying high-risk patients and understanding modifiable factors that shape care patterns, healthcare organizations can implement targeted interventions to reduce morbidity, mortality, and costs while promoting health equity.

Other Projects

Systematic Review of Diabetes-Prevention Interventions

Project Author: Syeda Maryam Abidi

Synthesizing evidence from randomized controlled trials of pharmacological, lifestyle, and digital-health interventions to prevent progression from prediabetes to type-2 diabetes — examining incidence and key metabolic outcomes across diverse populations to identify effective, scalable prevention strategies.

Cost-Effectiveness of GLP-1 Therapy for Prediabetes

Project Author: Syeda Maryam Abidi

Evaluating the cost-effectiveness of GLP-1 receptor-agonist therapy for high-risk individuals with prediabetes. Using a health-system perspective, the analysis compares long-term costs and outcomes versus standard care to inform payer coverage and diabetes-prevention policy.

Medicaid Gatekeeping & Access to Continuous Glucose Monitoring

Project Author: Syeda Maryam Abidi

Examining how state Medicaid programs govern access to continuous glucose monitoring through coverage rules. Using policy surveillance and legal-epidemiology methods, the study builds a typology of CGM gatekeeping and tests whether policy design is associated with diabetes outcomes.

Multi-Source Data Pipeline for Longitudinal Analysis

Project Author: Junchao Fei

Developing a multi-source data pipeline that links person-level administrative records into analyzable episodes, uses a rule-guided, LLM-assisted workflow to extract information from unstructured interview text, and assembles tract-level neighborhood measures for consistent linkage between individual records and community context.

Clinical & SDoH Data Integration for Equity Monitoring

Project Author: Junchao Fei

Building a practical framework that integrates clinical data and social determinants of health into a consistent, analysis-ready structure, then applying statistical analyses to track temporal trends, compare patterns across communities, and support equity-focused monitoring.
